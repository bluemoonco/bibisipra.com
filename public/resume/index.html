Summary

Bibi Sipra is a Certified Public Accountant and former Ernst & Young Tax Senior Manager who works as an independent contract and fractional corporate tax professional. She has more than ten years of corporate income tax experience: seven years at EY in Global Compliance & Reporting (Tax Staff, Tax Senior, Tax Manager, Tax Senior Manager) and, since 2023, project-based engagements with Big 4 firms and multinational companies.

Her specialties are ASC 740 accounting for income taxes (income tax provision, deferred taxes, valuation allowances, uncertain tax positions, income tax footnotes), US federal and multistate corporate income tax compliance, US international tax (foreign tax credit and Form 1118, Section 904 loss accounts, income sourcing, BEAT, FDII, GILTI, Forms 5471, 5472 and 8858) and Australian corporate tax for a multiple-entry consolidated (MEC) group. She also works under IFRS (IAS 12) for foreign-parented and private equity-owned groups.

Companies engage her for busy-season surge capacity, interim coverage of a Senior Tax Manager or Tax Director seat, post-acquisition and post-restructuring tax projects, provision insourcing, and technical special projects that would otherwise go to a Big 4 firm. Every contract engagement to date has ended with a full-time offer. She uses AI tools to build tax models and draft memos faster, and reviews and ties out every output herself.

Areas of expertise

Accounting for income taxes (ASC 740 and IAS 12)

Annual and quarterly income tax provision; effective tax rate analysis; deferred tax assets and liabilities; valuation allowance analysis and deferred tax asset reversal scheduling; uncertain tax positions (ASC 740-10, formerly FIN 48); income tax footnote and disclosure for Form 10-K, 10-Q and S-1 filings; purchase accounting and opening balance sheet tax entries; carve-out financial statements; stock-based compensation tax effects (ASC 718); return-to-provision true-ups; SOX 404 tax controls; PCAOB audit support; IFRS tax provision for foreign-parented groups.

US federal corporate tax compliance

Form 1120 consolidated returns; book-to-tax differences (Schedule M-3); fixed asset and depreciation (bonus, Section 179, MACRS); Section 263A UNICAP; Section 163(j) interest limitation; net operating loss and Section 382 limitation; consolidated return regulations (Reg. 1.1502) including attribute allocation on deconsolidation; accounting method changes (Form 3115); transaction cost analysis; estimated payments and extensions.

US international tax

Foreign tax credit and Form 1118 (all schedules); Section 904 limitation, overall foreign loss (OFL), overall domestic loss (ODL) and separate limitation loss (SLL) accounts; Section 861 expense allocation and apportionment; Section 863(b) sourcing of inventory sales; GILTI (Form 8992); Subpart F; FDII (Form 8993); BEAT (Form 8991); Section 267A hybrid rules; Forms 5471, 5472 and 8858; controlled foreign corporation reporting; transfer pricing coordination; Pillar Two awareness.

State and local income tax

Multistate corporate income and franchise tax compliance; apportionment and sourcing methodologies; nexus analysis; unitary and combined filing; separate-company states; state NOL tracking; state addbacks; look-back refund reviews; high-volume return review in ONESOURCE Income Tax.

Australian corporate tax

Multiple-entry consolidated (MEC) groups; allocable cost amount (ACA) cost-base resets; capital gains tax events (ITAA 1997); Subdivision 768-G participation exemption; Division 230 taxation of financial arrangements (TOFA); intercompany loan write-offs; restructuring add-backs; coordination with the ATO position and the group's Big 4 advisor.

Leadership and delivery
